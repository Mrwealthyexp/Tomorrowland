The Reclamation Ledger

Forgotten projects and erased chapters — what was taken from us, and the blueprint to build it back.

Tulsa, Oklahoma · 1921

Black Wall Street

Greenwood was the wealthiest Black community in America — banks, hotels, theaters, law offices. In two days it was burned to the ground by a white mob; 300 dead, 10,000 homeless, not one perpetrator prosecuted.

Lesson: Wealth without legal and political defense can be destroyed overnight. We rebuild with both.
Rosewood, Florida · 1923

The Rosewood Massacre

A thriving self-sufficient Black town erased in a week of racial terror. The survivors' descendants won reparations in 1994 — the first time a state compensated victims of racial violence.

Lesson: Documentation wins reparations. Archive everything.
New York City · 1857

Seneca Village

A prosperous Black landowning community razed by eminent domain to build Central Park. 1,600 residents displaced; the land is now worth billions.

Lesson: Land is leverage. Our land trust comes first, celebration second.
Wilmington, NC · 1898

The Only Coup on US Soil

A duly elected biracial government overthrown by force. Black officials expelled, the Black press burned, dozens killed — the only successful coup d'état in American history.

Lesson: Political wins must be defended institutionally, not just electorally.
Philadelphia · 1985

The MOVE Bombing

A city dropped a bomb on a residential block, killing 11 — including 5 children — and destroying 61 homes. No city official was ever criminally charged.

Lesson: State violence without accountability repeats. Memory is resistance.
Washington, DC · 1874

The Freedmen's Bank Collapse

60,000 newly freed depositors lost their savings when the Freedman's Savings Bank failed — a betrayal that seeded a century of distrust in financial institutions.

Lesson: We must own the rails. Community treasury, community custody.

Identity & Status — Reclaiming How We Are Named

From "Negro" to "Colored" to "Black" to "African American" — every label was assigned within a legal system that once classified us as property. This track studies the documented history of racial classification in US law and census records, examines indigenous identity claims and lineage research, and equips members to correct their records with evidence. Begin with the Academy's Black Economics Reading Path, then request the Identity & Status study kit via ORACLE (bottom right).

The Overlooked

Justice & remembrance for the lives mainstream media forgot — and a fund for the families still fighting.

Emmett Till
Money, Mississippi · 1955 · Age 14

Lynched after an accusation later admitted to be false. His killers confessed publicly after acquittal — protected by double jeopardy.

Killers never punished
Fred Hampton
Chicago, Illinois · 1969 · Age 21

Assassinated in his bed in a pre-dawn raid coordinated between Chicago police and the FBI while leading breakfast programs and a Rainbow Coalition.

State assassination
The 16th Street Girls
Birmingham, Alabama · 1963 · Ages 11–14

Addie Mae Collins, Cynthia Wesley, Carole Robertson, Carol Denise McNair — killed in the church bombing. One bomber walked free for 14 years; the last conviction came in 2002.

Justice delayed decades
Eleanor Bumpurs
Bronx, New York · 1984 · Age 66

A grandmother shot twice by police during an eviction over $96 in rent. The officer was acquitted; the city settled for $200,000.

No conviction
Yusef Hawkins
Brooklyn, New York · 1989 · Age 16

Surrounded and shot by a mob of up to 30 youths who mistook him for someone else. Most participants were never charged.

Most suspects never charged
The MOVE 11
Philadelphia · 1985 · 5 children among the dead

Eleven people, five of them children, killed when the city bombed a residential row house and let the fire burn 61 homes to the ground.

No criminal charges
